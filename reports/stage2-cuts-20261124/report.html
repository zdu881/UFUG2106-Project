Smaller circuits after variable-order search

UFUG2106 · Stage 2 · Group 2 · Zeyun DU (50025854) and Jincan Li (50032637) · 24 November 2026

Method

A gate shared by two outputs adds area once. A slow gate sets the circuit delay only when it lies on a longest path. We exploit these facts by sharing repeated Boolean functions and spending timing slack where it removes gates.

The submitted Stage 1 solver chose variable orders and mapped Shannon decompositions into AND2, OR2 and NOT gates. The integrated Stage 2 solver also compares output cones on a shared gate graph. A gate used twice costs area once. A bounded-memory path constructs a circuit when the full search cannot fit.

The new finishing pass uses the four-leaf SOP rewrite from the R10.0 research solver. A cut exposes up to four internal signals. Evaluating the old cone for every assignment to these signals yields at most 16 output bits. A sum of products covering the same bits can replace the cone in any surrounding circuit. The pass tries both polarities and ranks up to 160 roots. It rebuilds the live graph, then accepts a replacement only if weighted area falls and the circuit meets its delay target.

The finishing pass gets up to 90 ms. It skips positive memory limits, graphs above 20,000 gates and unsupported costs. Time checks are cooperative.

Generalization check

Thirteen public circuits landed exactly at the target. We tightened the target by 10% (rounded down) on 26 public functions where a saved reference proved feasibility and the original integrated circuit would miss. Re-synthesis met all 26 targets. Both solvers also met all targets on 24 new structured cases. One four-bit adder exposes a gap in feasible area: our circuit used area 961 at delay 20, while the generating circuit used area 152 at the allowed delay of 25. These checks motivate further work on shared intermediate functions across outputs.
